How I work

The same five steps, whether it's a loyalty report or a store network.

  1. 1

    Spot the pain

    Watch where people retype, wait, or guess. Store visits are where I find most of it.

  2. 2

    Measure it

    How many stores, how many hours, how often it goes wrong. Numbers decide what to fix first.

  3. 3

    Build with AI

    AI writes code with me. I decide the design, the rules, and what must never break.

  4. 4

    Test with real users

    The people who'll use it try it first. Their complaints become the next version.

  5. 5

    Pitch and roll out

    Show the before and after, package it as one file, and write the guide.

POS sales sync agent

Every store's sales sat on its own POS terminal. Head office needed them in one place for reports.

My part: I'm a collaborator on the company's sync agent, and since its original developer left, I maintain it. I handle its security, including a review that found 11 new issues, and I monitor it so it stays online. I also wrote the store troubleshooting guide and built about 30 SQL reports and data checks on top.

My idea, in all 31 stores

VPN watchdog

When a store's VPN dropped, sales stopped syncing until someone noticed. Store staff can't be asked to fix it.

What it does: checks the connection, reconnects the VPN by itself after three failed checks, and keeps exactly one sync agent running. Staff only see a message when they need to act.

PythonpywinautoPowerShell
Used by our tech engineers

Swirl Points Tool

Loyalty reports from stores arrive messy, and engineers retyped them by hand into a fixed email format.

What it does: checks every line for card, amount and duplicate receipts, asks for approval on the doubtful ones, and exports a clean Excel file. All 262 stores are built in.

PythonPyQt6Excel export
